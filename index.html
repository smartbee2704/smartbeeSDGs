Our History & Legacy

A Vision That Lives On

The SMART BEE SDGs Research Center was established in 2010 by Professor Dr. Panuwan Chantawannakul. Initially named “SMART Beekeepers,” the Center was founded with a vision of improving the quality of life of Thai beekeepers through multidisciplinary science, innovation, and community engagement.

Professor Panuwan earned her bachelor's degree from Chiang Mai University and her Ph.D. from Cardiff University, United Kingdom. She was a founding member of the COLOSS Association and later served as its Vice President. Recognized among the world’s top 2% of scientists in entomology, she was internationally renowned for her pioneering research on bee health and received numerous awards at both national and international levels.

Professor Panuwan passed away in March 2022, but her vision continues through her research, colleagues, students, and the communities she inspired. We remain committed to carrying forward her passion for bees and her dedication to using science to improve people's lives.

Bee Biology & Bee Health

Our research focuses on the biology, health, and ecological interactions of honeybees and other pollinators. We investigate bee physiology, immune responses, and the effects of pathogens, parasites, pesticides, and environmental stressors on bee health and survival.

Our studies also explore the diversity and transmission of bee pathogens, including viruses, bacteria, fungi, and microsporidian parasites, to better understand disease dynamics and their impacts on bee populations. In addition, our research focuses on disease monitoring and prevention, sustainable beekeeping practices, honey quality assessment, and the development of value-added bee products.

By integrating molecular biology, microbiology, and experimental approaches, we aim to improve disease detection and management, enhance beekeeping practices, and promote high-quality honey production. Through these efforts, we support healthy bee populations, sustainable beekeeping, and the conservation of pollinators and their essential ecological roles.

  • Bee Biology, Physiology & Immunity
  • Bee Health, Diseases & Parasites
  • Pesticide Toxicity & Environmental Stress
  • Pathogen Detection & Disease Management
  • Pollinator Health & Conservation
  • Honey Quality & Production
  • Sustainable Beekeeping & Product Development

Edible Insects

Our research explores edible insects as sustainable biological resources, focusing on their biology, diversity, development, nutrition, and potential applications. We investigate insect growth, development, and rearing practices to improve cultivation methods, production efficiency, and the sustainable utilization of insect resources.

Our work also examines the nutritional composition and potential benefits of edible insects, exploring their applications as alternative protein sources and ingredients for food and other value-added products. We also explore opportunities to improve insect utilizatio, with enhanced quality and practical applications.

By understanding insect biology and integrating scientific knowledge with practical approaches, our research aims to promote sustainable insect farming, improve production systems, and support the development of innovative insect-based products. Through these efforts, we contribute to sustainable food systems, efficient resource utilization, and the growing potential of edible insects as alternative food resources for the future.

  • Edible Insect Biology, Diversity & Development
  • Insect Growth, Rearing & Cultivation
  • Nutritional Composition & Potential Benefits
  • Sustainable Insect Farming & Production
  • Insects as Alternative Protein Sources
  • Insect-Based Food & Product Development
  • Value-Added Products & Resource Utilization
  • Sustainable Food Systems & Food Security

Microbiome & Microbial Ecology

Our research focuses on the diversity, composition, and ecological functions of microbial communities across various natural and human-associated environments. By integrating molecular techniques, next-generation sequencing, and bioinformatics, we investigate microbial community structures, ecological interactions, and the environmental factors shaping microbial diversity.

Our studies explore the relationships between microorganisms and their surrounding environments, including their roles in ecosystem processes, nutrient cycling, and biological interactions. Furthermore, we study gut microbiomes to understand microbial diversity and host–microbe interactions. Our microbial ecology research also explores microbial communities in soil, mining sites, and landfills, and we also investigate the functional potential of microbial communities and their responses to environmental changes. Through these approaches, our research aims to advance the understanding of microbial ecology and support the conservation, sustainable management, and utilization of microbial resources.

  • Next-Generation Sequencing & Bioinformatics
  • Microbial Functional Prediction
  • Environmental & Host-Associated Microbiomes
  • Microbial Community Dynamics
  • Microbial Diversity & Community Structure
  • Microbial Ecology & Interactions
  • Microbial Resource Conservation
  • Sustainable Utilization

Cultural Heritage Microbiology

Our research explores the relationships between microorganisms, biodiversity, ecosystems, and cultural heritage. We bring together microbial ecology, environmental microbiology, and conservation science to understand how microbial communities interact with their surrounding environments, heritage materials, and archaeological sites.

Our also investigate microbial communities associated with cultural heritage and archaeological sites, focusing on microbial diversity, community composition, environmental influences, and their interactions with heritage materials and surrounding environments. We examine how environmental conditions influence microbial growth and contribute to biodeterioration processes that may affect the preservation of cultural heritage. Through interdisciplinary research, we examine ecological processes associated with biodeterioration and provide knowledge to support the monitoring, conservation, and preservation of cultural heritage, archaeological materials, and their surrounding environments.

  • Microbial Ecology & Biodiversity
  • Microbial Community Analysis
  • Cultural Heritage Microbiology
  • Archaeological & Heritage Sites
  • Biodeterioration
  • Conservation & Preservation

Ethno-microbiology

Our research investigates microbial communities associated with traditional and indigenous fermented foods, particularly those of ethnolinguistic communities in Northern Thailand. Using next-generation sequencing and microbiome analysis, we explore microbial diversity, community structure, microbial interactions, and predicted functional potential associated with different fermentation practices.

Our studies integrate microbiology with traditional knowledge to understand how cultural practices and fermentation processes shape microbial communities. We also investigate beneficial microorganisms and potential microbial indicators related to food quality and safety, with the goal of supporting the conservation, improvement, and value-added development of traditional fermented foods. Furthermore, we explore the relationships between microbial diversity, fermentation conditions, and the unique characteristics of traditional food products. This research contributes to documenting local food heritage and identifying opportunities for sustainable food production and future applications of beneficial microorganisms.

  • Traditional Fermented Food Microbiome
  • Ethno-microbiology
  • Microbial Diversity & Community Structure
  • Microbial Interaction & Network Analysis
  • Functional Prediction & Beneficial Microorganisms
  • Food Quality, Safety & Sustainability

Tea Science

Our research and activities explore the science, history, and cultural heritage of tea, with a focus on tea chemistry, fermentation, brewing techniques, and sensory characteristics. We investigate the chemical compounds and biological processes that contribute to the flavor, aroma, and quality of different tea varieties, while exploring traditional and modern tea-processing methods.

Our interests also extend to the development of innovative fermented tea products, particularly those utilizing Thai Assam tea (Camellia sinensis var. assamica) and fermentation techniques inspired by traditional Pu-erh tea production. We also explore how microbial activity and fermentation conditions influence tea quality, flavor development, and product characteristics. Through research, product development, educational workshops, and cultural activities, we aim to integrate scientific knowledge with traditional tea wisdom, promote tea appreciation, and support the sustainable development of local tea resources.

  • Tea Chemistry & Bioactive Compounds
  • Tea Fermentation & Microbiology
  • Tea Processing & Product Development
  • Tea Brewing Science
  • Tea Sensory Evaluation
  • Tea History & Cultural Heritage
  • Tea Education & Workshops
