Our History & Legacy
Our History & Legacy

A Vision That Lives On

The SMART BEE SDGs Research Center was established in 2010 by Professor Dr. Panuwan Chantawannakul. Initially named “SMART Beekeepers,” the Center was founded with a vision of improving the quality of life of Thai beekeepers through multidisciplinary science, innovation, and community engagement.

Professor Panuwan earned her bachelor's degree from Chiang Mai University and her Ph.D. from Cardiff University, United Kingdom. She was a founding member of the COLOSS Association and later served as its Vice President. Recognized among the world’s top 2% of scientists in entomology, she was internationally renowned for her pioneering research on bee health and received numerous awards at both national and international levels.

Professor Panuwan passed away in March 2022, but her vision continues through her research, colleagues, students, and the communities she inspired. We remain committed to carrying forward her passion for bees and her dedication to using science to improve people's lives.

About Us

Bee & Edible Insects

Our research focuses on research and innovation in bees and edible insects, from biology, biodiversity, health, and ecology to sustainable production and utilization. We combine scientific research with practical applications to support beekeepers and edible-insect farmers in improving farming practices, product quality, and value-added products.

Our work includes bee health and disease monitoring, bee conservation and protection, sustainable beekeeping, and honey quality assessment to support healthy bee populations and high-quality honey production.

We also explore bee- and insect-associated microorganisms, nutrition, cultivation, and innovative insect-based products. Through research, technology, and collaboration, Smart Bee Lab aims to develop sustainable solutions that benefit farmers, consumers, communities, and the environment.

  • Bee Health & Disease
  • Sustainable Beekeeping
  • Honey Quality & Production
  • Product Development & Value Addition
  • Edible Insect Farming
  • Bee & Insect Associated Microorganisms

Bee Microbiome & Metagenomic Analysis

Our research explores the microbial communities associated with bees, with a particular focus on the gut microbiome and its relationships with bee health, disease, nutrition, and environmental factors. Using next-generation sequencing and microbiome analysis, we investigate bacterial community composition, microbial diversity, community shifts, and potential microbial functions under different biological conditions.

Our work also examines how pathogens and other stressors influence the bee-associated microbiome, providing insights into host–microbe interactions and their potential roles in maintaining bee health. This research contributes to a better understanding of bee microbial ecology and supports the development of microbiome-based approaches for bee health and sustainable apiculture.

  • Bee Gut Microbiome
  • Host–Microbe Interactions
  • Bee Health & Disease-Associated Microbiome
  • 16S rRNA Amplicon Sequencing
  • Microbial Diversity & Community Structure
  • Microbial Functional Prediction

Fermented Food Microbiome & Ethno-microbiology

Our research investigates microbial communities associated with traditional and indigenous fermented foods, particularly those of ethnolinguistic communities in Northern Thailand. Using next-generation sequencing and microbiome analysis, we explore microbial diversity, community structure, microbial interactions, and predicted functional potential associated with different fermentation practices.

Our studies integrate microbiology with traditional knowledge to understand how cultural practices and fermentation processes shape microbial communities. We also investigate beneficial microorganisms and potential microbial indicators related to food quality and safety, with the goal of supporting the conservation, improvement, and value-added development of traditional fermented foods.

  • Traditional Fermented Food Microbiome
  • Ethno-microbiology
  • 16S rRNA Amplicon Sequencing
  • Microbial Diversity & Community Structure
  • Microbial Interaction & Network Analysis
  • Functional Prediction & Beneficial Microorganisms
  • Food Quality, Safety & Sustainability

Environmental & Cultural Heritage Microbiology

Our research explores the relationships between microorganisms, biodiversity, ecosystems, and cultural heritage. We bring together microbial ecology, environmental microbiology, and conservation science to understand how microbial communities interact with their surrounding environments, heritage materials, and archaeological sites.

We investigate microbial communities associated with cultural heritage and archaeological sites, focusing on microbial diversity, community composition, environmental influences, and their interactions with heritage materials and surrounding environments. Through interdisciplinary research, we examine ecological processes associated with biodeterioration and provide knowledge to support the monitoring, conservation, and preservation of cultural heritage, archaeological materials, and their surrounding environments.

  • Microbial Ecology & Biodiversity
  • Microbial Community Analysis
  • Cultural Heritage Microbiology
  • Archaeological & Heritage Sites
  • Biodeterioration
  • Conservation & Preservation
